\section{Implementation Details}

\subsection{Retriever Details}

For a corpus of $N$ documents, inverse document frequency is computed as:
\[
\mathrm{idf}(t)=\log\left(\frac{1+N}{1+\mathrm{df}(t)}\right)+1,
\]
where $\mathrm{df}(t)$ is the number of documents containing term $t$. Query and document vectors are computed in the same TF-IDF space, and documents are ranked by cosine similarity:
\[
\mathrm{sim}(q,d)=\frac{q\cdot d}{\lVert q\rVert \lVert d\rVert}.
\]

\subsection{Evidence Compression Scoring}

For compact evidence modes, candidate sentences are scored as:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{score}(q,s,i) ={}& .40\,O(q,s)+.25\,R(q,s)\\
&+.20\,P(q,s)+.10\,D(q,s)\\
&+.05(1+i)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $O$, $R$, $P$, and $D$ denote unigram overlap, rare query-term coverage, phrase overlap, and token density. The position bonus mildly favors earlier sentences.

\section{Routing and Fallback Details}

TACER uses only query text, retrieval scores, and lexical evidence coverage; it does not use dataset identifiers or an additional LLM call before generation. The query-shape detector labels concise prompts as factoid unless the query contains multi-hop cues such as \emph{which}, \emph{where}, \emph{when}, \emph{whose}, \emph{both}, or \emph{and} and has at least six content words. Non-concise questions are treated as factoid-style questions when they contain a question mark, and otherwise as claim-style inputs.

Evidence complexity is assigned from general query features. A concise query is treated as distributed when it contains connective evidence or at least two capitalized entities and has at least six content words. Otherwise, any query with at least twelve content words, two connective cues, three capitalized entities, or two numeric expressions is treated as distributed; a query with at least seven content words or one capitalized entity is moderate; remaining queries are focused.

For concise short-answer prompts, fallback is triggered only for an empty answer or for an answer with more than 18 content words and answer-context overlap below 0.80. These concise-task rules are intentionally conservative because many correct answers are names, dates, places, or short phrases with limited lexical overlap.

\section{Ethics}

The experiments use benchmark-style datasets and generated answers. Because SciFact and BioASQ are biomedical, outputs should not be treated as medical advice or clinical evidence. We evaluate the system as an information retrieval and question-answering method, not as a clinical decision tool.

\section{Human Annotation Protocol}

The first annotation study validates automatic metrics against human factual judgments. It contains 120 outputs from SciFact, HotpotQA, and BioASQ, sampled across Llama-70B and Mistral. Two annotators agreed on 84 examples, yielding 70.0\% raw agreement and Cohen's $\kappa=0.477$. Table~\ref{tab:supp_metric_validation_labels} gives the agreed label counts.

The targeted TACER annotation set contains 15 HotpotQA and 15 ASQA queries. For each query, four saved Llama-70B outputs are shown in random order: Fixed Top-10, Adaptive-$k$ (paper), Safe Adaptive Context, and TACER. Annotators see only System A/B/C/D labels, the query, reference answer, model answer, and retrieved evidence. Method identities, automatic scores, and token counts are hidden during annotation. Two annotators label all 120 outputs. Exact four-way agreement is 68.3\% with Cohen's $\kappa=0.422$; binary acceptable/unacceptable agreement is 92.5\% with $\kappa=0.688$.
